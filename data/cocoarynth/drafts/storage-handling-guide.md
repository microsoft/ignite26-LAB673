## Keep it cool, dry and steady

| Condition | Target |
|---|---|
| Temperature | **15–20 °C** in storage, in transit and on display |
| Relative humidity | Below 60% |
| Light | Out of direct sunlight |
| Odors | Away from produce, coffee, cleaning products and fuel |
| Changes | Avoid quick moves from cold to warm |

## What is bloom?

**Bloom** is a whitish, grey or streaky film on the surface of chocolate. It is **safe to eat**, but bloomed bars look old and cannot be sold as first-quality product.

{{ photo("glossy-bar", "Well-tempered chocolate in good condition: glossy and even.", "half") }}

**Fat bloom (heat bloom).** When chocolate gets warm, some of the cocoa butter melts and moves to the surface, where it re-crystallizes as dull grey streaks and blotches. The risk rises sharply **above 25 °C**, even if the bars never look melted. A few hours in a hot truck, depot or shop window is enough.

**Sugar bloom.** When cold chocolate is moved into warm, humid air, moisture condenses on the surface, dissolves a little sugar and leaves a dry, white, dusty film when it evaporates. Avoid by letting cartons warm up slowly before opening.

{{ photo("sugar-bloom-bar", "Sugar bloom: dry, powdery white dusting with spots where condensation dried. Unlike fat bloom, it feels gritty, not greasy.", "half") }}

## Rules for carriers

1. Ship in refrigerated or insulated vehicles set to 15–20 °C.
2. **Never stage chocolate in unrefrigerated areas** such as overflow bays, docks or yards, even for "a few hours".
3. If a cold room is full, call Cocoarynth Logistics before moving the pallet.
4. Keep the temperature logger on the pallet and return it with the delivery paperwork.

## Rules for retailers

Store back-stock in a cool room, display away from windows and heat lamps, and report any bloom within 7 days with photos and the wrapper lot code.
