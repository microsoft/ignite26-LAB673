Cocoarynth has bought cacao from Esmeraldas, on Ecuador's northern Pacific coast, since 2018. Our partner there is the **Cooperativa Agroartesanal Río Claro**, a cooperative of 86 smallholder families that collects freshly harvested beans from its members, ferments and dries them at a central collection center, and exports them to us through Guayaquil.

{{ photo("cacao-pods-ecuador", "Ripening pods on a Río Claro member farm. The cooperative sells under the trade description Nacional.") }}

## The people

The collection center is run by **Marisol Vega**, who weighs every delivery, writes the collection ticket and assigns the beans to a lot. Each lot code (for example *EC26-L05*) follows the beans through fermentation, drying, export and our factory, so we can always tell which deliveries went into a bar.

## The farms we buy from

Río Claro has many members, but most of the beans Cocoarynth buys come from three farms:

| Farm | Planted area | Shade |
|---|---|---|
| Finca Las Brisas | 4.2 ha | Mixed shade: guaba, banana and laurel |
| Finca El Cedro | 3.6 ha | Young cedar and banana shade |
| Finca La Ceiba | 5.1 ha | Remnant forest canopy |

**Single origin does not mean single farm.** Our Ecuador bars are single-origin because every bean comes from Esmeraldas through Río Claro. A single lot may combine deliveries from two or more of these farms when they harvest in the same week; the collection tickets record exactly which.

## Post-harvest

- **Fermentation:** three-tier wooden boxes for six days, turned every 48 hours.
- **Drying:** raised wooden beds under a retractable roof for eight to ten days, until the beans reach about 7% moisture.

{{ photo("fermentation-boxes", "Tiered fermentation boxes under banana leaves.", "half") }}

## Flavor

Floral and jasmine aromas, dried fig and a mild cocoa base. These beans go into **Ecuador Río Claro 72%** and **Ecuador Río Claro 85%**.
